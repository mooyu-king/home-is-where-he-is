When home stops feeling familiar

Suzy Says takes a return home and changes what that familiar setting asks of you. An interactive DVD and a set of colored floor mats introduce an activity that resembles Simon Says, but the experience is first-person survival horror rather than a relaxed party game. The tension comes from having to interpret rules while the presentation makes concentration uncomfortable. Coming from Home Is Where He Is, you may recognize the broader appeal of a domestic place becoming unsettling. That comparison describes an editorial theme, not a shared story or connection between the games.

This companion is organized around decisions you can make before and during an opening session. It does not supply an invented solution to the DVD, a complete sequence of commands, or a route to a hidden ending. The useful starting point is smaller: understand the controls, recognize what the current instruction actually says, and know the limits of the browser adaptation. Familiarity with the childhood game may help explain the premise, but it should not replace attention to the rules that this particular game presents on screen.

Decide whether this is the right session

Choose a volume and screen position that you can comfortably change. Give yourself permission to leave without finishing an attempt. Respond to meaning rather than urgency

Simon Says style rules make the condition attached to an instruction important. A verb alone is not always sufficient reason to act. In Suzy Says, the horror presentation can make a quick reaction feel necessary even while careful listening remains important. Try to understand the whole command before selecting an action. That advice does not establish a fixed safe delay: any timing requirement shown by the game still applies. The aim is to avoid guessing from a fragment, not to ignore an instruction until a convenient moment.

Think of a response as three separate decisions: what was requested, under what condition, and which available input matches it. You do not need to narrate those decisions out loud or use the checklist during a timed scene. They are a way to prepare your attention. If a later command changes the pattern, update your understanding rather than forcing it into an earlier rule. This page does not disclose the complete internal rules, and it does not promise that a single memorized technique prevents every failure.
