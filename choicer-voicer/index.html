About The Choicer Voicer

Make the Room Quiet Enough to Hear

The Choicer Voicer makes a microphone session beginning with physical space rather than the bright studio interface part of play. A home session feels informal, yet recorded sound changes the social space. Somebody chooses the material, somebody controls the device, and somebody places a voice into a system that can replay or judge it. Clear setup keeps that vulnerability playful. The game-show graphics can provide a host and a panel, but the group must still decide how loud is comfortable, which impressions are welcome, and whether every participant agrees to be recorded.

A grounded method is to remove background audio and test ordinary speech. Use the mouse to select a mode and pack, then stop for a microphone check. Approve browser access, confirm the intended system input, click inside the player, and speak normally. Listen to the prompt once without talking. Break it into words, pace, pauses, pitch movement, emphasis, and emotional intention. Pick two features, perform from a consistent distance, and change one feature on the next take. Conversational volume usually preserves more detail than shouting and makes a shared room easier to manage.

When the group laughs, make sure the performer is laughing too; consent matters more than keeping the show moving. The developer describes the project as early access with limited built-in content and known microphone compatibility reports. Prepare a short pack from audio you have rights to use, test before guests arrive, and remember that local or streamer modes do not create online multiplayer. The Choicer Voicer FAQ

What is The Choicer Voicer?

The Choicer Voicer is a customizable microphone party game where vocal impressions, voice packs, computer judges, local multiplayer, streamer modes, and Dub Mode turn a room into a game-show studio.

How do I play?

Use the mouse for setup, approve microphone access, listen to a prompt, perform an impression, and compare the response.

Why is microphone permission needed?

Voice is the central input, so the browser and operating system must explicitly allow recording.

Why is the microphone silent?

Check browser permission, system privacy, selected input, other recording apps, iframe focus, and a basic stereo setup.

What is a voice pack?

It is a set of audio prompts. Use a small familiar pack containing only audio you created, licensed, or have permission to use.

How many people can play locally?

The published project page describes simple local multiplayer for up to four players.

What is Dub Mode?

Dub Mode lets players record voiceover performances over scenes, emphasizing timing and sustained character delivery.

Is online multiplayer included?

No. The developer states that online multiplayer is not currently available.

How should a group handle recording consent?

Explain what will be recorded, let anyone decline, avoid sharing files without permission, and delete unwanted takes.

How do I avoid voice strain?

Use conversational volume, keep sessions short, drink water, and stop when speaking becomes uncomfortable.
